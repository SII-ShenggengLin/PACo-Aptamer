## Introduction

Aptamers — short single-stranded nucleic acids that bind molecular targets with high affinity and specificity — have become workhorses of diagnostics, imaging, targeted delivery and synthetic biology, precisely because they combine antibody-like recognition with chemical synthesizability and programmable evolution. For RNA aptamers in particular, selections such as SELEX routinely yield binders to small molecules, peptides and proteins that are difficult to address by other modalities. Yet the discovery pipeline remains dominated by experiment: selection campaigns are slow, target-coupled and expensive, and computational sequence design could in principle compress the search — if it engaged with the property that defines an aptamer in the first place.

A growing family of methods designs RNA sequences conditioned on a three-dimensional backbone, reframing aptamer engineering as inverse folding. RhoDesign couples a geometric graph encoder with a transformer decoder; gRNAde demonstrated multi-state design on Eterna backbones; NA-MPNN extended the message-passing inverse-folding paradigm to nucleic acids; AlignIF added structure-alignment-driven evolutionary priors. These tools consistently improve sequence recovery — the fraction of positions recovered from the native sequence — and recovery correlates with measurable design quality. But a structural invariant is common to all of them: **the target is absent from the model**. Every method conditions on the RNA backbone alone; the ligand that the aptamers exist to bind enters neither training nor inference. The problem is stated plainly by the authors of gRNAde themselves, who single out "accounting for partner molecules with RNAs" as an open direction for the field. The one attempt to validate structure-only design on the binding axis — AlignIF's wet-lab follow-up — found that improved structural fidelity did not improve, and in cases degraded, binding affinity. Binding is the defining function of an aptamer, yet it has never been an explicit input to aptamer sequence design.

The omission is not conceptual but architectural, and the protein field has already shown the way out: LigandMPNN demonstrated that atomic ligand context, injected as tokens into a protein inverse-folding architecture, yields measurable gains exactly at the binding interface. RNA, however, compounds two difficulties. First, data scarcity: the PDB contains fewer than 900 RNA–small-molecule complexes, one to two orders of magnitude fewer than the structure or SELEX-sequence data available on the RNA side. Second, the information anchor: RNAbpFlow recently showed that base-pair structure is the most reliable conditioning signal for RNA generative modelling, but no method has combined secondary-structure conditioning with target context in the sequence-design direction.

Here we present **TACo-Aptamer** (Target-Atom-Conditioned aptamer design), which makes the ligand a first-class conditioning variable of RNA inverse folding: pocket atoms of the target are embedded as tokens (element identity plus distance geometry) and jointly attended alongside nucleotide representations during complex finetuning, while a predicted secondary-structure pairing map supplies complementary constraints. A two-stage strategy exploits the data asymmetry — pretraining on 6,988 RNA-only chains, then finetuning on complexes with ligand context — and an output ensemble (two finetuned checkpoints, stepwise probability averaging) provides the final configuration. Because recovery-style benchmarks cannot see interface effects that occupy 2–3% of sequence positions, we introduce a **quasi-blind evaluation protocol** with three layers of isolation — structure-cluster holdout (TM ≥ 0.45 ∪ CD-HIT 0.8 transitive closure), temporal splitting (2024+ structures), and, for the first time in this field, **ligand-level isolation** (test ligands entirely unseen in training) — and we evaluate with paired statistics, interface-recovery optics, secondary-structure fidelity, structural self-consistency against a recovery-matched negative control, and a 442-interaction retrospective audit across two public aptamer databases. Under this protocol TACo-Aptamer leads all four retrained baselines on every test set and both recovery optics (+1.9 to +5.5 percentage points over the strongest competitor, all 24 paired comparisons significant at P < 10⁻⁵), leads absolute interface recovery, and its designs remain diverse, novel and compositionally natural. The retrospective audit adds a calibrating message — the likelihood of a structure-conditioned model ranks real SELEX aptamers above structure-matched random sequences, yet cannot rank binding affinity within families: likelihood measures structural compatibility, a prerequisite of aptamer function rather than a predictor of it — arguing for the binding-centred evaluation protocols introduced here as the field's next step. TACo-Aptamer thus moves RNA aptamer design from a folding-centric to a binding-centric paradigm.

## Results

### A quasi-blind benchmark with ligand-level isolation

(Fig. 1; full protocol in Methods.) We first established what a fair comparison requires. All five methods — TACo-Aptamer, RhoDesign, gRNAde, NA-MPNN and AlignIF — were retrained from their official training loops on identical splits (5,773 training chains; method-specific featurization tolerances reduce effective sets by at most 3%, Extended Data Table 1), and evaluated on three non-overlapping test layers: a cluster-holdout test set (477 chains), a temporally isolated set (235 chains released in 2024 or later), and a zero-shot target set (93 chains whose ligands never occur in any training complex; 54 test + 39 independent chains). The zero-shot layer implements a form of isolation that, to our knowledge, no previous RNA design study has enforced: the model must design sequences for pockets whose chemistry it has never seen conditioned on. Chains discarded by clustering (511), multi-copy crystal artefacts, and a series of parsing subtleties that had silently inflated earlier baselines (multi-copy concatenated sequences, protein-trace chains, modified-nucleotide mappings) were repaired or removed and are documented for reproducibility (Methods; refs. data-quality audit).

### Comprehensive gains under strict isolation

(Fig. 2.) On the cluster-holdout test set, TACo-Aptamer (two-model ensemble, selected on validation data only) recovered 74.90% of native sequence positions under the pool-best optic and 60.40% under the t = 0.1 mean optic, exceeding the strongest baseline, RhoDesign (69.36% / 55.10%), by +5.54 and +5.30 percentage points (pp), with paired per-chain Wilcoxon tests significant at P = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴² and P = 1.9 × 10⁻²⁷ (Fig. 2b,c). Head-to-head per-chain comparisons (Fig. 2d,e) show TACo-Aptamer winning 76.3% of chains against RhoDesign and 94.5% against the next-best method; per-target deltas are positive for 76% of chains and the effect is not carried by a subset of easy targets (Fig. 2f). All 24 method × test-set × optic comparisons against the four baselines are significant (largest P = 3.7 × 10⁻⁶, on the zero-shot set under the t = 0.1 optic). The gains persist across chain-length strata (Fig. 2i) and, notably, the model knows when it is doing well: chain-level mean log-probability correlates with recovery at Spearman ρ = 0.516 (bootstrap 95% CI [0.44, 0.59]; Fig. 2h), within-chain correlations are positive for 84% of chains, and selecting the top 10% of samples by confidence enriches recovery by +1.46 pp — a practical screening signal developed further in Fig. 6e.

### Zero-shot generalization to unseen ligands

(Fig. 3.) The ligand-isolated zero-shot set asks whether ligand-aware training helps or hurts when the pocket chemistry is new. TACo-Aptamer remains first on both optics (76.06% / 62.82% versus RhoDesign 71.75% / 59.46%; Δ = +4.3 / +3.4 pp, P = 1.0 × 10⁻¹² / P = 4.6 × 10⁻⁷), and on the temporally isolated set (74.44% / 58.25%; Δ = +3.2 / +1.9 pp). The zero-shot advantage (+4.3 pp) is of the same magnitude as the cluster-holdout advantage, indicating that ligand-aware finetuning does not create a chemical-space crutch: the training-time interface calibration transfers to pocket geometries whose ligands were never seen.

### Where the ligand signal acts: interface optics and honest ablations

(Fig. 4.) Global recovery is structurally blind to interface effects: in our benchmark, ligand-contact residues (< 5 Å) occupy only 2–3% of sequence positions, so even a perfect interface mechanism would move global recovery by less than 1 pp. Three analyses therefore target the interface directly. First, component ablations under the released configuration (Fig. 4a; 300-sample pools): removing the ensemble (single model) costs 3.2 pp globally and 4.3 pp at the interface, and within the single-model family, removing SS conditioning, augmentation or ligand context each costs 0.6–0.8 pp globally (SS the largest) — at this pool size, no component's removal produces a significant interface-specific deficit in isolation, a negative we report rather than tune away. Second, per-nucleotide accuracy as a function of distance to the ligand (Fig. 4b) shows that three of the four baselines lose accuracy approaching the pocket (normalized deficits −4.9 to −11.2 pp at ≤ 5 Å), RhoDesign sits at zero (+0.3 pp) and TACo-Aptamer keeps a near-zero pocket deficit (−1.8 pp) while leading absolute interface recovery (59.6% versus 55.4%, +4.0 pp; P = 0.18 under the released configuration). The second shell (5–12 Å) is difficult for every method, a finding we report rather than tune away. Third, we evaluate what the designs *do* rather than what they match: secondary-structure fidelity of refolded designs (Fig. 4c) places gRNAde first (INF 0.627) — its graph features produce compensatory mutations that preserve pairing patterns even at low sequence identity — with TACo-Aptamer (0.281) above RhoDesign (0.248) and both bounded by native (1.0) and random (0.03) references. Recovery and SS-fidelity are thus demonstrably independent axes, and we present them as a two-judge coordinate system rather than a single scoreboard. Structural self-consistency (refold designs with RhoFold+, compare to native; Fig. 4f) confirms the same dissociation: the native-sequence upper bound is 0.466 scTM (48% pass TM ≥ 0.45), NA-MPNN designs lead self-consistency (0.325) while TACo-Aptamer leads recovery, and TACo-Aptamer's designs remain 3.8 pp above a recovery-matched random control (CI [2.4, 5.1], P = 4 × 10⁻⁶; Fig. 4g) — the matched control collapses to gRNAde's level (0.226 versus 0.219), demonstrating that recovered sequence content carries genuine fold information rather than composition-level artefacts.

### Generation quality: diversity, novelty and compositional naturalness

(Fig. 5.) A design method is only useful if its pools contain choices. At t = 0.1, TACo-Aptamer's pool diversity (mean pairwise edit distance 0.236) is 2.9× that of RhoDesign (0.082) and 88.2% of its samples are unique sequences (RhoDesign: 29.8%, indicating near mode collapse), while recovery is 5 pp higher; increasing temperature raises diversity further with only −1.2 pp recovery cost (Fig. 5a). Designs are not training-set memories: median maximum identity to the training library is 0.48 and only 2.1% of designs exceed 0.8 identity (Fig. 5b), with a similar distance to known SELEX aptamers in AptaDB (median ~0.50). Composition-space embedding of 60 test targets' design pools interleaves all methods with native sequences rather than forming isolated clusters (Fig. 5c), and family-conditional 4-mer profiles are matched most faithfully by TACo-Aptamer (KL 3.73 versus 4.31–4.64 for baselines; the random reference, 3.47, is a maximum-entropy anchor and is reported to preempt misreading, Fig. 5d). Folding-energy density of the designs sits closest to native statistics among all methods (MFE −0.258 versus −0.254 kcal mol⁻¹ nt⁻¹), with GC content modestly above native (0.57 versus 0.52; Fig. 5e).

### Case studies across ligand families and a retrospective audit of the recovery paradigm

(Fig. 6.) We first survey the breadth of the case evidence (Fig. 6a): twelve test chains spanning twelve distinct ligand identities — the purine series (xanthine, N2-acetylguanine, hypoxanthine, and guanine in complex with a methyltransferase ribozyme), the metabolite cofactors preQ1, SAM, ZMP, NMN and GTP, the aminoglycoside apramycin, and two selected-aptamer fluorophores (TAL1 and TO1-biotin) — and 22–119 nt in length. For each chain, the best TACo-Aptamer design (pool-best recovery) is refolded by RhoFold+ and superposed on the native crystal chain: wherever the refold predictor itself succeeds (scTM ≥ 0.4; five chains, versus one for RhoDesign), the redesigned sequence retraces the native tertiary fold and the pocket-contact residues track the bound ligand. Across the twelve chains, TACo-Aptamer leads pool-best recovery on 10 of 12 (median 74% versus 68%) and refold scTM on 10 of 12 with one tie (median 0.26 versus 0.18); we deliberately retain two chains where RhoDesign recovers more (6PRV_D, 6E8S_B), where recovery and fold self-consistency dissociate — the axis dissociation that Fig. 4f anticipates.

The depth case (Fig. 6b,c) is the N618-class fluorescent RNA aptamer released in June 2024: three chains (8HZD_A/B, 8HZE_A) lie in the intersection of temporal and ligand-level isolation — new structures, new-to-model ligands (the N618/N-series fluorophores; the GDP/GTP moieties in these entries are covalent 5′ caps rather than the bound ligand). TACo-Aptamer takes the best or tied-best recovery on all three chains (86.1 / 77.1 / 77.1%; mean 80.1 versus RhoDesign 77.4), and on 8HZD_A its best design recovers all seven ligand-interface positions (< 5 Å of N618; Fig. 6c). To probe the binding axis computationally we assembled what is, to our knowledge, the largest retrospective audit of model likelihood against real aptamer data: 442 RNA–target interactions with measured affinities from two independent databases (AptaDB, 214 clean interactions; UTexas aptamer database, 228), each sequence scored under its own RhoFold+-predicted structure and secondary structure, against length-matched random controls sharing the identical structural conditioning. Real SELEX aptamers score +1.95 log-units *above* their matched random controls — every one of the 442 interactions ranks above random (per-database +2.01 and +1.90; Fig. 6d) — so the model's likelihood does recognize genuine aptamer sequences as more compatible with their structures than random sequences. Affinity, however, is a different quantity: within families, likelihood–Kd correlations are null (19 families, mean ρ = 0.11, not significant), so likelihood separates folded aptamers from noise but does not rank binding strength. Four diagnostics support the robustness of both statements (predicted versus experimental structures agree to +0.003 log-units per nucleotide; borrowed-structure controls, GC stratification (ρ = 0.02), G-quadruplex filtering). Recovery-type and likelihood-type metrics, in other words, measure structural compatibility — a prerequisite for aptamer function, not a predictor of it — which is why interface optics and binding-centred evaluation of the kind used above are needed to go further. Finally, on the practical question of triaging large design pools (Fig. 6e), per-design secondary-structure fidelity is the strongest single ranking signal (+2.27 pp recovery at 1% budget, above log-probability at +1.77 pp), while naive rank-averaging of the two anti-correlated signals is actively harmful (−2.47 pp) — consensus construction is itself a methodological choice, and an oracle ranking (+14.1 pp) quantifies the headroom for learned triage.

## Discussion

We have shown that the target — the molecule an aptamer is defined by — can be brought inside RNA inverse folding as atomic pocket context, and that a two-stage, dual-conditioned architecture trained on less than 900 complexes converts this into consistent benchmark leadership under the strictest isolation protocol yet applied to this problem, including the first ligand-level zero-shot evaluation. The interface is where any ligand-channel effect must live — global recovery mathematically cannot see 2–3% of positions — and there TACo-Aptamer holds a near-zero pocket deficit where learning-based baselines lose 5–11 pp; we also report, as a boundary of the current configuration, that isolated component ablations at 300-sample resolution do not individually reach significance, so the ligand channel's contribution is presented as design rationale calibrated by interface optics rather than as a single-module effect.

Several limitations bound these claims and chart the next steps. First, we provide no wet-laboratory validation; our functional evidence is computational and, deliberately, bidirectional — a recovery-matched negative control and a self-consistency upper bound delimit what structure-prediction optics can certify, and the 442-interaction retrospective audit shows that likelihood recognizes real aptamers as structure-compatible yet cannot rank their affinities. The honest reading is that interface conditioning is a calibrated inductive bias, not a binding predictor, and closing the gap requires binding information inside the training objective — for example, learned or physics-based interface scoring terms, contrastive objectives against mismatched targets, or negative design — which our architecture is positioned to absorb (the target-token channel accepts any pocket chemistry, and we verified zero-architecture-change injection of protein pockets on 30 RNA–protein complexes, though with no measured gain until protein complexes enter finetuning). Second, the chemical space of training ligands is dominated by a few families (SAM, ATP, flavins); the zero-shot set probes but does not exhaust extrapolation, and we report it as a protocol, not a guarantee. Third, secondary-structure fidelity and structural self-consistency dissociate from recovery across methods — gRNAde and NA-MPNN respectively lead those axes — reinforcing that no single judge suffices; the multi-judge protocol introduced here (recovery, interface optics, SS-fidelity, self-consistency with matched controls, retrospective audit) is offered as reusable infrastructure, together with the splits, canonical truth sets and evaluation scripts we release. Looking forward, the natural extensions are conformational ensembles as conditioning (induced fit is the physical reality of aptamer binding), negative design against related off-targets, and protein-target finetuning on the thousands of available RNA–protein complexes. Aptamer design has spent its first decade learning to fold sequences back onto structures; the results here indicate that the next decade can be spent conditioning on what those structures are for — moving RNA aptamer engineering from folding-centric to binding-centric design.

## Methods

### Benchmark construction and data quality

**Source data.** All RNA-containing PDB entries (24,252 entries; download August 2025 unless stated) were parsed into chain-level records; 6,988 RNA-only chains form the pretraining pool and 868 RNA–small-molecule complexes form the finetuning pool. Functional ligands were identified by CCD whitelist (605 ligand identities; standard buffers, ions and crystallization agents excluded), and interface residues defined as any nucleotide atom within 5.0 Å of a ligand heavy atom.

**Splits with three layers of isolation (splits_v2).** Sequence clustering (CD-HIT-est 0.8, word size 4) and structure clustering (US-align, TM ≥ 0.45) were combined by transitive closure into 300 equivalence classes, split 8:1:1 into training (5,773 chains), validation (96) and cluster-holdout test (477); 511 chains were discarded by cluster-size and quality rules. Two additional test layers are disjoint from training by construction: an independent temporal set (235 chains first released in 2024 or later) and a zero-shot target set (93 chains — 54 from test, 39 from independent — whose ligands never occur in any training complex; the finetuning ligand set and the zero-shot ligand set have zero intersection, and finetuning chains have zero overlap with all three test sets). Thresholds follow field precedent: TM 0.45 matches the AlignIF clustering protocol and sits far above the random-pair TM average (~0.17); CD-HIT 0.8 with -n 4 is the recommended nucleic-acid setting and closes the TM blind spot for same-sequence/multiple-conformation crystals.

**Data-quality audit.** A reproducibility audit preceded all comparisons. Multi-copy crystal chains stored as concatenated sequences (40 chains, including 3 test chains) were rebuilt as single-copy all-atom PDBs and a canonical truth set (`canonical_v2`, 6,346 chains) regenerated; 29 protein-trace chains were removed from baseline training sets; modified nucleotides were mapped to parent bases with a documented mapping; legacy atom names normalized; N-undetermined positions excluded from all denominators. Official code paths required for local execution were patched and version-locked: an AlignIF inference bug that invalidated 19 of 20 samples per chain on chains with numbering gaps; NA-MPNN parser failure modes (missing C1′ residues, insertion-code counting); RhoDesign NMR multi-model inputs. Five chains with missing C1′ coordinates are evaluated on valid positions only. All patches are documented in the released repository.

### TACo-Aptamer architecture

TACo-Aptamer is an encoder–decoder sequence designer with two conditioning channels. **RNA geometry.** Each nucleotide contributes nine atoms (C4′, C1′, C2′, C3′, N1/N9, C2, C5′, O5′, P); a geometric vector perceptron (GVP) encoder (3 layers; scalar 512, vector 256) builds node embeddings over k-nearest-neighbour edges with rotation-frame-invariant features (dihedrals, sugar pucker proxies), and the predicted base-pairing map contributes pairwise edge features. **Ligand context.** Every pocket atom within 5.0 Å of the RNA becomes a token: element one-hot plus a 16-bin radial basis embedding of its distance pattern to nucleotide frames, passed through a two-layer MLP into the model dimension. Ligand tokens are concatenated to the nucleotide sequence and jointly attended by a 3-layer transformer encoder (512 dimensions, 4 heads); a 3-layer autoregressive transformer decoder generates the sequence position by position at a chosen temperature, cross-attending the mixed context so that every decoding step sees the pocket. The architecture therefore injects the target as atomic context with no ligand-specific vocabulary — any element combination, including protein backbone atoms, is admissible without modification.

**Training.** Stage 1 pretrains on 6,988 RNA-only chains (structure-to-sequence, masked-token cross-entropy, coordinate-noise and random-rotation augmentation, SS-dropout 0.15 variant retained; 60 epochs, batched by length buckets). Stage 2 finetunes on the complex pool with ligand tokens active (8 epochs, validation-selected, mixed 1:1 with stage-1 chains against forgetting). The released configuration, *ens2*, averages the stepwise softmax probabilities of two stage-2 checkpoints (seed 43 and SS-dropout 0.15) before multinomial sampling — pool sizes are unchanged relative to single models (fair-budget comparison; inference cost ×2, reported) and the choice was made on validation data only. At benchmark inference all methods, TACo-Aptamer included, condition on the backbone and predicted secondary structure only; the ligand channel acts during finetuning, and its effect is assessed through interface optics and the channel ablation of Fig. 4a (inference-time pocket injection was separately verified to add ~1 pp at the interface, not significant, once the backbone is given). Ablation variants (no SS conditioning; no ligand context; no augmentation) retrain the finetuning stage identically from the same stage-1 checkpoint.

### Baselines

RhoDesign, gRNAde, NA-MPNN and AlignIF were retrained from their official training loops on the identical 5,773-chain training split with official hyperparameters and validation-based checkpoint selection (effective training-set sizes: 5,683 / 5,584 / 5,679 / 5,733, reflecting featurization tolerances). For inference, every method draws from pools of identical budget: three temperatures (0.1, 0.5, 1.0) × 100 sequences for TACo-Aptamer, RhoDesign and gRNAde; AlignIF (t = 0.1 × 20) and NA-MPNN (t = 0.1 × 10) follow their official inference defaults, and pool-best optics therefore mildly disadvantage them — the t = 0.1 mean optic is reported alongside precisely to control for pool size.

### Evaluation optics and statistics

**Recovery.** Pool-best (per chain, the pool member maximizing identity to the native sequence; chains averaged) and t = 0.1 mean (per-sample recovery averaged within the t = 0.1 pool, then across chains). Unknown-base (N) positions are excluded from denominators; baseline outputs shorter than native (NA-MPNN's 5 C1′-deficient chains, AlignIF's dash-filled positions) are aligned by difflib and scored on valid positions. **Interface optics.** Interface recovery (ligand-contact positions), interface-minus-overall difference (intra-method contrast, robust to the small-interface saturation that inflates absolute values — most benchmark interfaces span 1–2 residues), and per-nucleotide recovery binned by distance to the nearest ligand atom, both absolute and normalized per chain (bin minus chain mean). **SS fidelity.** Designs are refolded with RNAfold (2.7.2) and compared to the input pairing map by INF = TP/√(NR·NP); the input map is itself the MFE prediction of the native sequence (native control INF = 1.000, verifying the self-consistency of this convention). **Self-consistency.** Designs and native sequences are refolded with RhoFold+ (single-sequence mode; 1,149 predictions, zero failures) and scored by scTM against the native structure (US-align, TM normalized by native length); the recovery-matched random control mutates each TACo-Aptamer design away from native while preserving per-chain identity, then passes it through the identical pipeline. **Retrospective audit.** 442 measured-affinity RNA interactions (AptaDB crawl, 1,350 interactions filtered to RNA/ACGU/length 15–300/deduplicated; UTexas Zenodo 8387047, 1,495 rows identically filtered), reduced by CD-HIT-est 0.8 against the full training library to 214 + 228 clean interactions; each sequence scored by teacher-forced ensemble log-probability under its own predicted structure and SS, against 10 length-matched random-sequence controls sharing the same structure and SS. **Generation quality.** Diversity (mean normalized edit distance), unique fraction, max identity to training library and to AptaDB (edlib Needlemen–Wunsch with 4-mer prefiltering), 4-mer composition t-SNE, leave-one-out TM-cluster family profiles (KL), GC and MFE density (RNAfold). **Statistics.** Paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests per chain; 95% confidence intervals by 1,000–5,000× bootstrap; no multiple-comparison correction was applied given the confirmatory, pre-registered-optics structure of the comparisons. Code, splits, canonical truth sets, model weights and per-chain results are released.

## Abstract (final)

RNA aptamers are selected to bind molecular targets, yet every computational method for designing aptamer sequences conditions on RNA structure alone — the target never enters the model, and improved structural fidelity has been shown not to translate into improved binding. Here we present TACo-Aptamer, which brings the target inside RNA inverse folding: ligand pocket atoms are embedded as tokens and jointly attended alongside geometric nucleotide representations and a predicted secondary-structure pairing map during finetuning on 868 RNA–ligand complexes, after pretraining on 6,988 RNA-only chains. Because interface positions occupy only 2–3% of sequence, we introduce a quasi-blind benchmark with ligand-level isolation — test ligands are entirely unseen in training — alongside cluster holdout (TM ≥ 0.45) and temporal splits, all methods retrained on identical data. TACo-Aptamer leads four strong baselines across all six test-set × metric combinations (+1.9 to +5.5 percentage points over the strongest competitor; all 24 paired comparisons significant, P ≤ 3.7 × 10⁻⁶), generalizes to unseen ligands (76.1% pool-best recovery versus 71.8%), and keeps a near-zero per-nucleotide deficit at the ligand interface (+4.0 pp absolute interface recovery) where learning-based baselines lose 5–11 pp. Designs remain diverse (2.9× baseline diversity at matched recovery), novel (median training-set identity 0.48) and compositionally native. A 442-interaction retrospective audit across two aptamer databases shows that model likelihood ranks real SELEX aptamers above structure-matched random sequences (+1.95 log-units, 442/442) yet does not rank binding affinity within families — likelihood captures structural compatibility, a prerequisite for binding rather than a predictor of it — motivating the binding-centred evaluation protocol and interface optics released with this work. TACo-Aptamer thus moves RNA aptamer design from folding-centric towards binding-centric paradigms.
